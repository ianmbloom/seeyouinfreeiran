# Iran Executed Dissidents — Quick Reference Table

| # | Name | Era | Why Prominent | Fate | Portrait |
|---|------|-----|---------------|------|---------|
| 1 | **Sadegh Ghotbzadeh** | 1979–1982 | Khomeini's close aide in Paris exile; became Iran's Foreign Minister during the US hostage crisis. One of the Revolution's most visible faces who grew disillusioned and plotted against Khomeini. Executed by the man he helped bring to power. | Firing squad, Evin Prison, Sept. 15, 1982, age 46 | [Portrait, 1980](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sadegh_Ghotbzadeh,_press_conference_-_1980_(cropped).jpg) |
| 2 | **Shapour Bakhtiar** | 1979–1991 | The Shah's last Prime Minister; a lifelong socialist democrat who tried to steer Iran toward constitutional government as the Revolution erupted. Fled to Paris, became the most prominent opposition leader in exile, and was sentenced to death in absentia by Khalkhali in 1979. | Stabbed to death by Iranian agents in his Paris home, Aug. 6, 1991, age ~77 | [Portrait, Tehran 1979](https://media.gettyimages.com/id/120668028/photo/chapour-bakhtiar-in-tehran-iran-in-february-1979.jpg) |
| 3 | **The 1988 Massacre** | 1988 | Khomeini issued a secret *fatwa* ordering the execution of all political prisoners who remained "steadfast." "Death Commissions" toured 32+ prisons. Between 2,800 and 30,000 prisoners — mostly MEK members and leftists — were hanged in the span of weeks. Mass graves remain unmarked. | Mass extrajudicial execution, July–Sept. 1988. UN concluded in 2024 it was carried out with *genocidal intent*. | — |
| 4 | **Dariush Forouhar** | 1998 | Veteran nationalist politician, co-founder of the Pan-Iranist Party, and former Labor Minister under Mossadegh. He and his wife refused to flee after the Revolution and kept holding open democratic meetings in their Tehran home for 20 years. | Stabbed 11 times by Ministry of Intelligence agents in his home, Nov. 22, 1998, age 70 | [Portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/01/Dariush_Forouhar_photo.jpg) |
| 5 | **Parvaneh Forouhar** | 1998 | Human rights activist and co-leader of the Pan-Iranist Party alongside her husband Dariush. Their murders triggered the exposure of the decade-long state program of assassinating secular intellectuals known as the "Chain Murders." | Stabbed 24 times in the same attack as her husband, Nov. 22, 1998, age 59 | [Portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a1/01-ParvanehEskandariForouhar.jpg) · [Together](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Dariush_And_Parvaneh_Forouhar.jpg) |
| 6 | **Fereydoun Farrokhzad** | 1992 | Celebrity TV host, singer, and entertainer — brother of Iran's greatest modern poet, Forough Farrokhzad. Beloved throughout pre-revolutionary Iran for his flamboyant charisma. In exile in Germany, he became an outspoken critic of the regime. Tracked for years by Iranian intelligence. | Stabbed to death in his Bonn apartment, Aug. 7, 1992, age 53. In 2025, a former IRGC minister admitted the IRGC paid foreign agents to carry out such killings. | [Portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f8/Fereydoun-Farrokhzad.jpg) |
| 7 | **Neda Agha-Soltan** | 2009 | Philosophy student and musician shot by a Basij sniper during the Green Movement protests against Ahmadinejad's fraudulent re-election. A bystander filmed her dying in the street; the video went viral within hours. *Time* called it "probably the most widely witnessed death in human history." She became the global face of Iranian resistance. | Shot in the chest by a Basij militiaman, Tehran, June 20, 2009, age 26 | [Portrait](https://en.gariwo.net/i2/202006241042_neda-1.jpg?v=20210320195153) |
| 8 | **Zahra Kazemi** | 2003 | Iranian-Canadian freelance photojournalist arrested outside Evin Prison for photographing families of detainees during the 2003 student protests. Subjected to brutal torture in custody — skull fracture, rape, brain damage. Her case caused Canada to downgrade diplomatic relations with Iran. No one has ever been convicted of her murder. | Died from torture in Iranian custody, July 11, 2003, age ~47 | [CPJ photograph](https://cpj-photos.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/zahra_kazemi_iran_2003.jpg) |
| 9 | **Mohammad-Reza Ali-Zamani** | 2009–2010 | Pro-monarchy activist and the first person sentenced to death in connection with the 2009 Green Movement protests. His actual role in the uprising was minimal — passing news to an opposition radio station. Denied access to a lawyer; his attorneys were not informed of his execution until after it was carried out. | Hanged alongside 20-year-old Arash Rahmanipour, Evin Prison, Jan. 28, 2010, age ~38 | [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mohammad-Reza_Ali-Zamani_(cropped).jpg) |
| 10 | **Navid Afkari** | 2018–2020 | Iranian national wrestling champion arrested in connection with 2018 economic protests. Alleged his confessions were extracted under torture. Before his execution he smuggled an audio message out of prison pleading his innocence. Both President Trump and the International Olympic Committee publicly appealed for his life — Iran executed him anyway, secretly, without notifying family. | Secretly hanged, Sept. 12, 2020, age 27. Family not notified. | [Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/champion-wrestler-2020-09-11-at-141238-1444x680.jpeg) |
| 11 | **Mohsen Shekari** | 2022 | The first person executed as a direct result of the 2022 "Woman, Life, Freedom" uprising — Mahsa Amini's protest movement. Arrested after a street protest, tried in under three weeks, hanged at 22. The speed of his execution — designed to terrify the protest movement while it was still active — shocked the international legal community. | Hanged, Dec. 8, 2022, age 22 | [Iran Human Rights](https://iranhumanrights.org/wp-content/uploads/Mohsen-Shekari-en.jpg) |
| 12 | **Majidreza Rahnavard** | 2022 | Second protester executed during the Woman, Life, Freedom uprising — and the first executed publicly, hanged from a crane in Mashhad in an act of deliberate mass intimidation. His entire trial lasted one session. From conviction to public hanging: two weeks. Amnesty called it a "revenge killing." | Publicly hanged from a crane, Mashhad, Dec. 12, 2022, age 23 | [BBC portrait](https://c.files.bbci.co.uk/6F1F/production/_127974482_mediaitem127974478.jpg) |
| 13 | **Ramin Hossein-Panahi** | 2017–2018 | Young Iranian Kurd from Javanroud arrested for alleged membership in the Kurdish opposition group PJAK. His case drew huge international attention; executions were delayed multiple times by global campaigns. During one near-execution, his niece Nishtiman committed suicide after security forces terrorized his family. Executed alongside cousins Zaniar and Loghman Moradi. | Hanged, Raja'i Shahr Prison, Sept. 8, 2018, age ~22–23 | [Iran Human Rights](https://iranhr.net/media/content/images/ramin_2.jpg.885x520_q85_box-0%2C0%2C884%2C520_crop_detail_upscale.jpg) |
| 14 | **Behnam Mahjoubi** | 2018–2021 | Member of Iran's Sufi Gonabadi dervish minority, arrested after a 2018 crackdown on a large dervish protest. Suffered from severe epilepsy which prison authorities deliberately denied treatment for. Months of torture and medical neglect. Transferred to hospital after collapsing; died in a coma. The regime claimed he self-poisoned. | Died in custody from torture and medical neglect, Feb. 21, 2021, age ~35 | [Iran HRM portrait](https://iran-hrm.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Behnam-Mahjoubi.jpg) |
| 15 | **Maryam Akbari Monfared** | 2010–present | Three brothers and a sister were executed in the 1988 massacre. In 2016, she filed a formal complaint demanding investigation — from *inside* Evin Prison, where she was serving a 15-year sentence. The regime's response was to deny her medical care and extend her imprisonment. Considered one of the most extraordinary acts of defiance in the Islamic Republic's history. | Still alive; imprisoned continuously since 2010, sentence repeatedly extended | [NCRI portrait](https://www.ncr-iran.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Maryam-Akbari-Monfared-400.jpg) |
| 16 | **Saleh Mohammadi** | 2026 | National-level freestyle wrestling champion, 19 years old. Arrested January 15, 2026 — one week after the bloodiest days of the January uprising. Accused of killing a police officer; his family, coaches, and CCTV evidence supported his alibi. Denied independent lawyers. Told the court his confession was made "under torture." Sentenced to death in under three weeks. Turned 19 in prison on March 11. Publicly hanged one week later. His last Instagram post: *"And we stood stronger than we ever imagined for ourselves."* | Publicly hanged, Qom, March 19, 2026 — eight days after his 19th birthday | [BBC portrait](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/news/480/cpsprodpb/77b3/live/a5999fe0-23d9-11f1-b297-95b0a0a8331e.jpg.webp) |
