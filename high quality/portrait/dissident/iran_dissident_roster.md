# Iran Dissident Roster
### Executed & Slain Dissidents of the Islamic Republic
*Game Reference Document — Carmen Sandiego-Style Card Profiles*

---

> **Research Note:** The Islamic Republic has used execution systematically as a political tool since 1979. Charges are typically framed as *moharebeh* ("waging war against God"), *baghi* (armed rebellion), or "spreading corruption on earth" — language used to criminalize dissent, protest, and minority identity. Confessions are routinely extracted under torture. This roster spans the full history of the republic, from the revolutionary purges of 1979 through the January 2026 uprising.

---

## ERA I — THE REVOLUTIONARY PURGES (1979–1985)

---

### 1. Sadegh Ghotbzadeh
**Born:** February 24, 1936, Tehran  
**Died:** September 15, 1982 (executed by firing squad, Evin Prison)  
**Age at death:** 46  
**Charge:** "Plotting the assassination of Khomeini and overthrow of the Islamic Republic"

**Biography:**  
A Western-educated intellectual and close personal confidant of Ayatollah Khomeini during his exile in France, Ghotbzadeh served as Director of state television and then as Foreign Minister during the 1979–1981 Iran hostage crisis — one of the most visible faces of the new Islamic Republic to the outside world. He was charming, secular in lifestyle, and genuinely believed the Revolution could become a liberal democracy. He was wrong. As Khomeini consolidated theocratic power, Ghotbzadeh grew disillusioned and began conspiring with military officers and clerics (including a son-in-law of Ayatollah Shariatmadari) to assassinate Khomeini and establish a "real republic." Arrested on April 8, 1982, he was subjected to severe torture before a forced televised confession. After a 26-day trial, he was shot by firing squad in Evin Prison. He was 46. More than 70 associates were executed around the same time.

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Well-photographed: wore open-collar shirts, had thick dark hair and a mustache; Western, urbane appearance  
- Multiple clear press conference photos from 1979–1980 available  
- Photogenic, cinematic face — strong contrast image for pixel art  
- Reference: [Wikimedia Commons portrait (1980)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sadegh_Ghotbzadeh,_press_conference_-_1980_(cropped).jpg)

**Game Card Tags:** `Insider Turned Dissident` `Foreign Minister` `Firing Squad` `Evin Prison` `1982`

### 2. Shapour Bakhtiar
**Born:** 1914 (or 1916), Lordegan, Iran  
**Died:** August 6, 1991 (assassinated in Paris by Iranian agents)  
**Age at death:** ~77  
**Charge:** Sentenced to death *in absentia* (1979); later murdered abroad

**Biography:**  
The last Prime Minister of the Shah's government, Bakhtiar was a French-educated lawyer, socialist, and lifelong democrat who agreed to serve in a last-ditch attempt to steer Iran toward constitutional government as the Revolution was exploding. He failed — Khomeini arrived in Tehran while Bakhtiar was still PM. He fled to Paris, where he became the most prominent Iranian opposition figure in exile for a decade. In May 1979, the "Hanging Judge" Sadegh Khalkhali sentenced him to death in absentia. On August 6, 1991, three assassins from Iran's intelligence services broke into his home in Suresnes, a Paris suburb, and stabbed him and his secretary to death. One assassin was caught and convicted in France. In 2025, a former IRGC minister confirmed that the IRGC coordinated assassinations of dissidents abroad.

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Silver-haired, elegant elderly statesman; round glasses in later years  
- Multiple wire service photos from 1979 (Tehran) and 1980s (Paris exile)  
- Distinguished, patrician face; suit and tie — contrast against revolutionary imagery  
- Reference: [Getty portrait, Tehran 1979](https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/news-photo/chapour-bakhtiar-in-tehran-iran-in-february-1979-portrait-news-photo/120668028)

**Game Card Tags:** `Last PM of the Shah` `Exile` `Paris Assassination` `IRGC Hit` `1991`

## ERA II — THE CHAIN MURDERS & SYSTEMATIC DISAPPEARANCES (1988–1998)

---

### 3. The 1988 Massacre — Unnamed Thousands
**Dates:** July–September 1988  
**Killed:** Estimated 2,800–30,000 (sources vary widely)  
**Location:** Prisons across at least 32 cities in Iran

**Biography (Collective):**  
In the summer of 1988, Supreme Leader Khomeini issued a secret *fatwa* ordering the execution of all political prisoners who remained "steadfast" in their beliefs — primarily members of the Mojahedin-e Khalq (MEK/MKO) and leftist groups (Fedaian, Tudeh Party). "Death Commissions" of three officials toured prisons asking prisoners a single question: *"Are you a Muslim?"* Those who answered wrong were hanged — in groups, often dozens per day, their bodies buried in secret mass graves. Grand Ayatollah Montazeri, heir apparent to Khomeini, protested in writing that "the greatest crime committed in the Islamic Republic" was being committed in his name. He was stripped of his position. In 2024, UN Special Rapporteur Javaid Rehman concluded the killings were carried out with *genocidal intent*. The perpetrators — including Ebrahim Raisi, later President of Iran — faced no accountability.

**Game Card Tags:** `Mass Execution` `Secret Fatwa` `Death Commissions` `1988` `Mass Grave`

### 4. Dariush & Parvaneh Forouhar
**Born:** Dariush — 1928; Parvaneh (née Eskandari) — March 20, 1939  
**Died:** November 22, 1998 (stabbed to death in their Tehran home)  
**Ages at death:** Dariush 70, Parvaneh 59  
**Charge:** None — murdered by intelligence agents

**Biography:**  
One of the most beloved couples in Iranian civil society. Dariush was a veteran nationalist politician, founder of the Pan-Iranist Party, and former labor minister. Parvaneh was a fierce human rights advocate and party co-leader. Together they represented a secular, democratic Iran. They refused to flee after the Revolution and continued holding open meetings in their Tehran home despite years of harassment. On November 22, 1998, Ministry of Intelligence agents broke in and stabbed Dariush 11 times and Parvaneh 24 times. Their murders sparked the exposure of the "Chain Murders" (*ghatl-haye zanjireh-i*) — a decade-long government program of assassinating secular intellectuals and writers. The mastermind, deputy intelligence minister Saeed Emami, "committed suicide" in custody by allegedly drinking a bottle of hair remover. Their daughter Parastoo has kept their memory alive from exile.

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Iconic couple portrait widely circulated — he had white hair and a thick mustache; she wore her hair back with a distinctive dignified bearing  
- Reference: [Wikimedia Commons couple portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Dariush_And_Parvaneh_Forouhar.jpg)  
- Two characters in one card — unusual; consider dual portrait pixel art

**Game Card Tags:** `Chain Murders` `Couple` `Stabbed in Home` `1998` `Nationalist` `Human Rights`

### 5. Fereydoun Farrokhzad
**Born:** September 29, 1938, Tehran  
**Died:** August 7, 1992 (stabbed to death in Bonn, Germany)  
**Age at death:** 53  
**Charge:** None — murdered by Iranian intelligence abroad

**Biography:**  
The brother of Iran's greatest modern poet, Forough Farrokhzad, Fereydoun was himself a celebrated entertainer — a flamboyant TV host, singer, and comedian beloved by Iranians before the Revolution, known for his charisma and his boundary-breaking performances. After 1979, he went into exile in Germany, where he became an outspoken critic of the Islamic Republic, using his celebrity platform to denounce the regime and advocate for democracy. Iranian intelligence tracked him for years. On August 7, 1992, he was found stabbed repeatedly in his Bonn apartment. For decades the murder was officially unsolved. In 2021, Radio Free Europe/Radio Farda investigations identified Iranian intelligence suspects. In March 2025, former IRGC minister Mohsen Rafiqdoost admitted that the IRGC paid Basque separatists to carry out assassinations of dissidents abroad — placing Farrokhzad's murder in direct state-sponsored context.

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Flamboyant, glamorous appearance; known for distinctive stylish outfits  
- Multiple performance photographs available  
- Reference: [Wikimedia Commons photo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f8/Fereydoun-Farrokhzad.jpg)

**Game Card Tags:** `Singer & Entertainer` `Exile` `Assassinated Abroad` `Germany 1992` `IRGC Assassination`

## ERA III — THE GREEN MOVEMENT CRACKDOWN (2009–2013)

---

### 6. Neda Agha-Soltan
**Born:** January 23, 1983, Tehran  
**Died:** June 20, 2009 (shot by Basij sniper during protest)  
**Age at death:** 26  
**Charge:** None — killed in the street

**Biography:**  
A philosophy student and aspiring musician, Neda was participating in the protests against the fraudulent re-election of Mahmoud Ahmadinejad when a Basij paramilitary member shot her through the chest on a Tehran street. A mobile phone video of her death — her eyes going vacant as blood spread across her face — was uploaded immediately to the internet and within hours was seen by tens of millions of people worldwide. Time magazine called it "probably the most widely witnessed death in human history." She became the face of the Green Movement and of Iranian resistance to the world. The Iranian government initially claimed she had been killed by protesters, or by the CIA, or by her own music teacher. The Guardian Council refused to investigate. She was 26 years old.

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Young woman; her face is one of the most photographed in protest history  
- Dark hair, light complexion; multiple clear portrait photographs  
- Reference: [Gariwo Foundation portrait](https://en.gariwo.net/i2/202006241042_neda-1.jpg?v=20210320195153)

**Game Card Tags:** `Green Movement 2009` `Symbol of Resistance` `Shot in Street` `Sniper` `Viral Martyrdom`

### 7. Zahra Kazemi
**Born:** 1955, Shiraz, Iran  
**Died:** July 11, 2003 (died from torture while in Iranian custody)  
**Age at death:** ~47–48  
**Charge:** "Photographing outside Evin Prison"

**Biography:**  
An Iranian-Canadian freelance photojournalist based in Montreal, Kazemi was arrested on June 23, 2003, while photographing families of prisoners waiting outside Evin Prison during student protests. She was taken inside Evin and subjected to brutal torture — her skull was fractured and she suffered brain damage from beatings. Transferred to Baghiatollah Hospital, she fell into a coma and died on July 11. The Iranian government initially denied culpability, then claimed she had died of a stroke. A reformist member of parliament who viewed her body reported she had been raped and tortured. The Iranian intelligence officer charged with her "semi-intentional murder" was acquitted by an Iranian court in 2004. Canada downgraded diplomatic relations. Her son, Stephan Hachemi, has fought for 20+ years to bring her killers to justice. As of 2025, no one has been held accountable.

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Middle-aged woman with glasses; photojournalist appearance  
- She is photographed both at work and in portrait; passport-style photos exist  
- Reference: [CPJ photograph](https://cpj-photos.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/zahra_kazemi_iran_2003.jpg)

**Game Card Tags:** `Journalist` `Iranian-Canadian` `Tortured in Custody` `Evin Prison` `2003`

### 8. Mohammad-Reza Ali-Zamani
**Born:** ~1972  
**Died:** January 28, 2010 (executed by hanging, Evin Prison)  
**Age at death:** ~38  
**Charge:** *Moharebeh* (enmity against God) — "taking up arms against Iran's Islamic system"

**Biography:**  
An activist with the pro-monarchy Kingdom Assembly of Iran, Ali-Zamani was arrested during the aftermath of the 2009 Green Movement protests, becoming the first person from those mass demonstrations to be sentenced to death. His actual role in the protests appeared minimal — he worked passing information to opposition radio stations. He was accused of visiting Iraq and meeting US military officials, which he denied. His lawyers were not informed of his execution until after the fact — a standard regime practice. He was executed alongside 20-year-old Arash Rahmanipour in a double hanging on January 28, 2010. Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch condemned both executions as politically motivated, warning they would "pave the way for further politically driven executions."

**Game Card Tags:** `Green Movement 2009` `Monarchist` `Executed 2010` `Show Trial` `Evin Prison`

## ERA IV — THE WOMAN LIFE FREEDOM UPRISING (2022–2023)

---

### 9. Mohsen Shekari
**Born:** February 24, 2000  
**Died:** December 8, 2022 (executed by hanging)  
**Age at death:** 22  
**Charge:** *Moharebeh* — blocking a road and injuring a Basij member with a knife

**Biography:**  
The first person executed as a direct result of the 2022 Mahsa Amini / "Woman, Life, Freedom" uprising. Shekari was 22 years old, arrested after a September 25 protest in Tehran's Sattar Khan district, accused of blocking a road and wounding a Basij member with a machete. His trial lasted less than three weeks from conviction to execution — a speed that shocked legal observers worldwide. He appealed; the Supreme Court upheld the sentence on November 20; he was hanged on December 8. Amnesty International said the charges did not legally justify the death penalty under international law, as the crimes did not constitute "intentional killing." His execution was designed to terrorize the protest movement while it was still active in the streets.

- Young man, 22; typical Iranian young adult appearance  
- Photographed in state media footage and rights organization images  
- Reference: [Iran Human Rights photo](https://iranhumanrights.org/wp-content/uploads/Mohsen-Shekari-en.jpg)

**Game Card Tags:** `Woman Life Freedom 2022` `First Execution` `22 Years Old` `Sham Trial` `December 2022`

### 10. Majidreza Rahnavard
**Born:** June 16, 1999  
**Died:** December 12, 2022 (publicly hanged from a crane)  
**Age at death:** 23  
**Charge:** *Moharebeh* — allegedly stabbing two Basij militia members during protests

**Biography:**  
The second protester executed during the Woman Life Freedom uprising, and the first to be executed publicly — hanged from a crane in Mashhad in an act of deliberate terror theater. His single trial session before a Revolutionary Court lasted one day, on November 29. He was convicted on December 12 — the entire process from trial to hanging took two weeks. Human rights groups noted that execution by crane in a public space is specifically designed to maximize psychological trauma on the population. Rahnavard was 23. His family was not allowed to collect his body immediately. Amnesty International stated the execution exposed "Iran's judiciary for what it is: a tool of repression."

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Young man; widely photographed before and at trial  
- Strong images of the crane execution location exist (use for background reference, not the act)  
- Reference: [BBC portrait](https://c.files.bbci.co.uk/6F1F/production/_127974482_mediaitem127974478.jpg)

**Game Card Tags:** `Woman Life Freedom 2022` `Public Hanging` `Crane Execution` `23 Years Old` `Mashhad`

### 11. Ramin Hossein-Panahi
**Born:** 1995, Javanroud, Iranian Kurdistan  
**Died:** September 8, 2018 (executed, Raja'i Shahr Prison)  
**Age at death:** ~22–23  
**Charge:** *Moharebeh* — alleged membership in Kurdish opposition group PJAK

**Biography:**  
A young Iranian Kurd arrested in January 2017 in Javanroud, accused of membership in PJAK (Kurdish Free Life Party), an armed Kurdish opposition group. His trial was found by international rights groups to be "grossly unfair" with serious torture allegations. His execution was delayed multiple times due to international campaigns — on one occasion when the date was announced, his niece Nishtiman committed suicide after security forces subjected the family to an intense intimidation campaign. Ramin was executed alongside cousins Zaniar and Loghman Moradi on September 8, 2018. All three maintained their innocence. Amnesty International called the executions "an outrage." Ramin's case highlighted Iran's systematic targeting of the Kurdish minority under vague security charges.

### 12. Navid Afkari
**Born:** July 22, 1993, Shiraz  
**Died:** September 12, 2020 (secretly executed)  
**Age at death:** 27  
**Charge:** Murder of a security guard during 2018 protests (charge disputed)

**Biography:**  
An Iranian wrestling champion — national-level competitor — arrested in connection with the 2018 "Dirty Water" economic protests in Shiraz. He was accused of murdering a security guard, a charge he and his family denied and which rights groups found dubious. Afkari alleged his confessions were extracted under torture. Before his execution, he managed to smuggle an audio message out of prison: *"My name is Navid Afkari and I am an Iranian citizen. I have been sentenced to death for a crime I did not commit."* US President Donald Trump and the International Olympic Committee directly appealed to Iran to spare him. Iran executed him secretly, in the early hours of September 12, 2020, without notifying his family or lawyers — a procedural violation even by Iranian law. He was 27.

## ERA V — THE JANUARY 2026 UPRISING

---

### 13. Saleh Mohammadi
**Born:** March 11, 2007, Qom  
**Died:** March 19, 2026 (publicly hanged, Qom)  
**Age at death:** 19  
**Charge:** *Moharebeh* and murder of a police officer during January 2026 protests

**Biography:**  
A national-level freestyle wrestling champion who had won a bronze medal at the 2024 Saitiev Cup, Saleh Mohammadi was 18 years old when he was arrested on January 15, 2026 — seven days after the most violent days of the January uprising, when Iranian security forces killed thousands of protesters in the streets. He was accused of participating in the killing of a police officer in Qom on January 8. His family, coaches, and teammates insisted he was at his uncle's house when the incident occurred, and CCTV footage apparently did not show him at the scene. The court denied his family's request to hire independent lawyers, assigned state lawyers instead, and based its verdict on confessions Mohammadi told the court had been extracted "under torture." His hands had been fractured during beatings. He was sentenced to death on February 4, less than three weeks after his arrest. He turned 19 in prison on March 11. He was publicly hanged on March 19 — one week after his birthday — alongside Mehdi Ghasemi (21) and Saeed Davoudi. The execution sparked international condemnation. US President Trump condemned it, echoing global outcry over a teenager with a coerced confession being killed in the street. His last Instagram post before his arrest showed him returning to training after an injury: *"And we stood stronger than we ever imagined for ourselves."*

**Pixel Art Notes:**  
- Very young man; athletic build, wrestler  
- Photographs from wrestling competitions and trial footage available

### 14. Erfan Kiani
**Born:** Unknown  
**Died:** April 25, 2026 (executed, Isfahan)  
**Age at death:** Unknown  
**Charge:** "Collaboration with Israeli Mossad" + acts of protest in Isfahan

**Biography:**  
Executed on April 25, 2026, one of the first January 2026 protesters executed for what authorities framed as foreign intelligence collusion — a charge routinely used by Iran to delegitimize protesters and justify maximum sentences. Authorities said Kiani led a group in Isfahan that damaged property, set fires, used Molotov cocktails, and attacked security forces, describing him as a "key figure" working for the Mossad. ## SPECIAL PROFILES

---

### 15. Maryam Akbari Monfared
**Born:** December 14, 1976  
**Status:** Imprisoned (15-year sentence, serving continuously since 2010)  
**Charge:** *Moharebeh* — "enmity against God"

**Biography:**  
A living emblem of the 1988 massacre's continuing legacy. Three of Monfared's brothers and one of her sisters were executed during the 1988 mass killings. In October 2016, from inside Evin Prison's women's ward, she filed an unprecedented formal complaint with the Tehran Prosecutor General demanding investigation into her siblings' executions. The regime's response was to deny her medical treatment and increase her restrictions. She remains imprisoned. Her courage in filing a formal complaint against state massacre — from *inside* the prison where her family was killed — is considered one of the most extraordinary acts of dissent in the Islamic Republic's history. The UN Working Group on Forced or Involuntary Disappearances has called on Iran to investigate her family's disappearances.

**Game Card Tags:** `1988 Massacre` `Living Prisoner` `Evin Prison` `Family Massacre` `Prisoner of Conscience`

### 16. Behnam Mahjoubi
**Born:** ~1985  
**Died:** February 21, 2021 (died in custody after torture and denial of medical care)  
**Age at death:** ~35–36  
**Charge:** "Propaganda against the system" — arrested for participating in a Sufi dervish protest

**Biography:**  
A member of Iran's Sufi Gonabadi dervish minority — a mystical Muslim order that has been systematically persecuted by the Islamic Republic. Mahjoubi was arrested in 2018 following the crackdown on a large Gonabadi protest. He suffered from severe epilepsy, which was ignored by prison authorities. While imprisoned in Evin, he was subjected to months of torture and denied medication and medical treatment despite urgent appeals from his family and international rights organizations. He was transferred to Loghman Hospital after collapsing, and died on February 21, 2021. Iranian authorities claimed he had poisoned himself with medication. Rights groups rejected this account. He was one of dozens of dervishes imprisoned in a coordinated campaign against the Gonabadi community.

**Game Card Tags:** `Sufi Dervish` `Religious Minority` `Died in Custody` `Torture` `Evin Prison` `2021`

## GAME DESIGN NOTES

### Suggested Card Eras / "Decks"
1. **The Revolution's Children** (1979–1985) — those devoured by the revolution they helped make
2. **The Disappeared** (1988) — the summer massacre; cards could be blank silhouettes
3. **The Writers & Artists** (1988–1998) — Chain Murders era; intellectuals targeted by intelligence
4. **The Green Generation** (2009–2013) — student protesters, journalists, democracy activists
5. **The Athletes** (2018–2022) — wrestlers Afkari and Rahnavard; sports as contested territory
6. **The January Uprising** (2026) — the most recent martyrs, executed publicly as deterrence

### Common Charge Vocabulary (for card flavor text)
- **Moharebeh** — "waging war against God" — most common death penalty charge
- **Baghi** — "armed rebellion against the Islamic Republic"
- **Efsad-e Fel-Arz** — "spreading corruption on earth" — used against religious minorities and intellectuals
- **Monafeqin** — "hypocrites" — the regime's term for MEK members and, sometimes, any dissident

### Pixel Art Portrait Guidance
- All subjects have publicly available photographs from news agencies, rights organizations, or Wikipedia
- Faces tend to be expressive, with strong contrast; Iranian features translate well to pixel art
- Consider era-appropriate palettes: muted earth tones for 1979–1988; cooler greens for 2009 Green Movement; high-contrast urgent reds for 2022 and 2026
- Athletes (Afkari, Rahnavard, Mohammadi) could be shown in athletic context before switching to their "wanted" card
- The Forouhar couple is an opportunity for a dual portrait card — unusual and powerful

BIOGRAPHY:
Nika Shakarami wore all black. She drew skulls. She went through a phase of
cutting her hair short and dyeing it blonde. She was obsessed with true crime,
anime, and the darkness at the edges of things. She found her German girlfriend
on Instagram because they were both posting about the same California satanist
serial killer. She sang her Persian bedtime songs over WhatsApp at night.

She also had a voice that stopped rooms. A video circulated worldwide of her at
a garden party, legs planted wide, gripping a microphone, belting out "Soltane
Ghalbha" — a classic Persian love song from a 1968 film — with total fearless
abandonment. A woman singing alone in public is technically forbidden in Iran.
She did not appear to care. "Part of my heart tells me: Go. Another part tells
me: Stay."

Her father died when she was 10. She worked hard to make ends meet. She and her
girlfriend made plans to finally meet in person.

On September 20, 2022 — four days after Mahsa Amini died in morality police
custody — Nika went to the protests near Laleh Park in central Tehran. She stood
on a dumpster, held her hijab over her head, and set it on fire. Undercover IRGC
operatives in the crowd flagged her as a protest leader. She was detained
approximately an hour later, loaded into an unmarked freezer van, sexually
assaulted and beaten to death by members of IRGC Team 12 before reaching Evin
Prison. Her body was cleaned of blood and dumped under a highway overpass. The
government claimed she jumped from a building.

The men responsible were not punished. One received a written reprimand.

She was sixteen years and eighteen days old. She would have turned seventeen
on October 2.
